02 — The Voluntary Stack
Five Products, One Purpose: Cash When It Matters
Every one of these pays a benefit directly to the employee. It doesn't go to the hospital or the doctor — it's theirs to use however they need it.
Life & AD&D
Replaces income for the family when an employee dies. AD&D doubles the payout for accidental death or dismemberment.
Typical benefit: 1–2× salary or flat $25K–$50K
STD & LTD
Replaces a chunk of the paycheck when an employee can't work — pregnancy, surgery recovery, extended illness, an accident.
Typical benefit: 60% of salary, STD 26 weeks, LTD to age 65
Accident
Pays a fixed cash amount for a covered accident — ER visit, fracture, stitches, concussion, torn ACL, surgery.
Example: broken leg → ~$1,200–$2,500 cash
Critical Illness
Pays a lump-sum check the day a covered illness is diagnosed — cancer, heart attack, stroke, organ failure.
Typical benefit: $10K, $20K, or $30K at diagnosis
Hospital Indemnity
Pays a flat amount when someone is admitted, plus a daily amount for every day they stay. Indemnity = cash paid on an event, not a reimbursement.
Example: $1,000 admission + $200/day
Why five, not one? Each product covers a different kind of event. Most employees end up picking two or three based on what fits their family.

04 — The Paycheck Question
Employee-Paid vs. Employer-Contributed
A $5–$10/paycheck employer contribution — less than a team lunch — more than doubles participation and changes how the benefit lands.
100% Employee-Paid
No Cost to Employer
Avg. participation rate38%
Employer monthly cost / ee$0
Perceived valueLow — another deduction

The classic voluntary setup. Zero cost to the company, but participation stays low because employees view it as another paycheck deduction to opt into.
Recommended

Bigger lift

Splitting the cost signals the benefit matters. Employees actually enroll, use it when they need it, and remember it at review time.
The math, roughly: 50 employees × $10/paycheck × 26 paychecks = ~$13,000/year — in exchange for a benefit package that lands as a genuine perk instead of a form in the enrollment packet.
