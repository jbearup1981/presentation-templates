02 — Anatomy
One fixed monthly payment · three buckets
$X,XXX / mo
Your monthly check. Fixed for the whole plan year. Carrier spreads claims into 12 equal payments — you never get a surprise bill mid-year.
SPLITS INTO →
Bucket 1
Admin Fee
~12–15%
Pays the carrier — claims processing, network access, member services, ID cards, the app. Fixed cost. Doesn't refund.
Bucket 2 · The Big One
Claims Fund
~70–75%
Reserved for your group's actual medical claims. If claims come in under this number, the surplus refunds to you.
Bucket 3
Stop-Loss Insurance
~12–18%
Bundled into your monthly check. Caps your downside — if a single claim or the group total blows past the limit, the carrier eats it.
✓
Plan year ends — we reconcile. If claims came in under your funded total, the carrier writes you a refund check (or credits next year's premium). Typical good-year refunds: 10–25% of claims fund.

Who level-funded works for

No major ongoing catastrophic claimants. The medical questionnaire and ZIP-code data drive the rate — healthier groups get better quotes and bigger refund potential.

02 — Honest Truth
When you should stay fully insured
We don't pitch level-funded to every group. If any of these are true today, fully insured is the safer answer — and we'll tell you that.
✗
Under 15 enrolled employees
The math doesn't work. A single bad claim distorts the whole group, stop-loss premium eats too much of the monthly check, and most level-funded carriers won't even quote you. Stay fully insured until you're north of 25 enrolled.
✗
Brand-new group with no claims history
Carriers price unknowns conservatively. Without 12–24 months of claims data, your quote will be loaded for risk — and you'll likely overpay vs. fully insured. Run fully insured for a year, then look again.
✗
Tight cash flow or mid-year budget constraints
Yes, monthly is fixed — but a "bad year" means no refund. If your budget is built on the assumption you'll get a refund check, that's the wrong reason to switch. Refunds are upside, not a forecasting line.
