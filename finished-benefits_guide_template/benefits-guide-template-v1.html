Medical · How to Use It [Company Name] · [Plan Year]
How It Works

How do I actually use my plan?

A quick walkthrough of what to do when you need care, what to bring, and how to keep your costs low.

Carry your ID card

You'll get a physical card and a digital one in the [Carrier] app. Show it at every visit. Photo of the card on your phone works too.

Find an in-network doctor

Search the [Network Name] network on [Carrier].com or in the app. Out-of-network costs are much higher — almost always worth staying in-network.

Preventive care is free

Annual physical, mammogram, colonoscopy, well-child visits, immunizations — all covered at 100%. Use them. They're already paid for.

Use telehealth first

For most non-emergencies, a virtual visit is faster and cheaper than urgent care. See page 14 for free 24/7 virtual care.

Check before a procedure

For anything planned (surgery, MRI, specialist), call [Carrier] member services at the number on your card to confirm coverage and get a cost estimate.

Save your EOB

After a claim, you'll get an Explanation of Benefits — it shows what was billed, what the plan paid, and what you owe. Match it to your bill before paying.

Copay vs. coinsurance A copay is a flat dollar amount ($25 for a doctor visit). Coinsurance is a percentage (you pay 20%, the plan pays 80%). Most plans use both, depending on the type of care.
Out-of-pocket max The most you'll pay in a year. Once you hit it, the plan pays 100% for the rest of the year. Includes your deductible, copays, and coinsurance — but not your monthly premium.

Voluntary

Accident Insurance

Critical Illness

Critical Illness

Pays a lump sum (typically $5,000–$30,000) if diagnosed with cancer, heart attack, stroke, kidney failure, or other serious conditions. Money is yours — use it for any expense.

Best for: family history of major illness
Hospital

Hospital Indemnity

Pays a flat amount for each day you're hospitalized — typically $100–$300 per day. Helps offset the deductible and out-of-pocket costs from your medical plan.

Best for: HDHP enrollees, expecting parents
Why these are different Your medical plan pays the doctor. These plans pay you. The cash can cover deductibles, lost income, child care, travel — anything you decide. No claim forms for the doctor; you submit a short claim and money comes to you (often in days).

When Things Change

If you lose eligibility.

If you leave [Company Name], have a reduction in hours, or experience another qualifying event, you have options to keep your coverage.

COBRA Continuation

COBRA lets you keep your medical, dental, and vision exactly as they are — same plans, same network, same providers — for up to 18 months (sometimes longer). The catch: you pay the full premium (employer + employee portion) plus a 2% admin fee.

Qualifying Life Events

These let you change your benefits outside open enrollment — but you only have 30 days to act.

  • · Marriage or divorce — add or drop a spouse
  • · Birth, adoption, legal guardianship — add a new dependent
  • · Death of a dependent
  • · Loss of other coverage — your spouse's plan ends, you age off a parent's plan
  • · Gain of other coverage — spouse's open enrollment, new job
  • · Change in employment status — full-time to part-time, return from leave
  • · Medicaid / CHIP eligibility change (60-day window for these)
Notify HR within 30 days — and bring documentation (marriage license, birth certificate, prior coverage termination letter). Miss the window and you wait until next open enrollment.

Enrollment

How to enroll in 6 steps.

All elections happen in [Enrollment Platform]. Plan to set aside 20–30 minutes the first time. You can save and come back.

Log in to [Enrollment Platform]

Go to [platform-url.com]. Use your work email and the password from your welcome email. New hires: HR will send setup instructions.

Required Notices [Company Name] · [Plan Year]
Required by Law

Required notices.

Federal law requires us to provide these notices each year. Full versions are available from HR — request a copy anytime.

  • Summary of Benefits and Coverage (SBC)

    A standardized 4-page summary of each medical plan — what's covered, what you pay, sample cost scenarios. Required by the ACA. Available at no cost from HR or in [Enrollment Platform] alongside each plan election.

    ACA
  • HIPAA Privacy Notice

    Describes how the plan and its business associates use and disclose your protected health information (PHI), and your rights as a participant. Provided at enrollment and re-issued every 3 years.

    HIPAA
  • Children's Health Insurance Program (CHIP) Notice

    If you or your dependents are eligible for Medicaid or your state's CHIP program, you may qualify for premium assistance. The federal CHIP notice details state-by-state contact information and 60-day special enrollment rights.

    CHIP
  • Medicare Part D Creditable Coverage Notice

    Confirms whether the prescription drug coverage in your medical plan is "creditable" — meaning at least as good as standard Medicare Part D. Important if you or a covered dependent is Medicare-eligible.

    Medicare
  • Women's Health and Cancer Rights Act (WHCRA)

    Group health plans that cover mastectomies must also cover reconstructive surgery, prostheses, and treatment of physical complications — to the same extent as other covered conditions.

    WHCRA
  • Newborns' and Mothers' Health Protection Act

    Group plans generally may not restrict hospital stays after childbirth to less than 48 hours (vaginal delivery) or 96 hours (C-section) — and may not require pre-authorization for these stays.

    NMHPA
  • Marketplace Notice

    You have an alternative to employer-sponsored coverage through the Health Insurance Marketplace at HealthCare.gov. You may qualify for a tax credit there if our plan does not meet ACA affordability or value standards (in most cases, it does).

    ACA
  • COBRA Initial Notice

    Required at the start of plan participation. Describes your right to continue health coverage if you, your spouse, or your dependents lose eligibility. Full COBRA details on page 17 of this guide.

    COBRA
